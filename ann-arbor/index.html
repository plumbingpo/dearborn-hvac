When Your System Fails in Ann Arbor

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer fast, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll reach a real person who will get a technician headed to your property quickly. Arrival in the area is usually within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Since our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls are wrapped up in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

Two common situations call for quick action from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

How Ann Arbor Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.
